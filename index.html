Your language e-reader.

Read real books in the language you're learning. Words you know stay in the original language. Words you don't get translated inline. Knowledge follows you across every book you ever read.

It's a companion, not a whole course. Logia won't drill grammar or replace your teacher, textbook, or favourite app — it does one thing well: it makes reading real books approachable, so you pick up vocabulary in context, almost without noticing.

How it works

Logia tracks every word you've seen, tapped, and marked. Each word has a confidence score that nudges up when you turn a page past it without checking, and down when you tap to peek. The page renders each word by how well you know it.

Three ways to read

Pick the mode that fits where you are — a toggle at the top of every page. Logia never switches for you; it only offers a gentle nudge when a mode clearly no longer fits.

  • Beginner — the page reads in your own language, and the words you're ready for are swapped in from the target language, more and more as your vocabulary grows.
  • Intermediate — the real target text, with short inline translations on the hard phrases and clauses, so you stay in the story without stalling.
  • Proficient — the target text with only a light gloss on the few words you're still unsure of.

Tap any word

A tap opens a card with the word's dictionary meaning, an example from the book, its CEFR level, on-device pronunciation, and the grammar that matters for its part of speech — verb conjugations, noun case and gender, articles and pronoun tables. One tap marks it known (or not), and that sticks.

Knowledge persists across books

Words you mastered in your first book come pre-known in your second. A short calibration quiz figures out your level in about 20 questions, then you start reading. Book two is measurably easier than book one — that's the whole idea.
