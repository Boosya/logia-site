Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 August 2026

Logia is built to keep your data on your device. This policy explains exactly what is collected, what isn't, and who has access to what. If anything here is unclear, email ekaterina.gasparian@gmail.com.

Data that stays on your device

All of your reading state lives in files on your phone and is never uploaded to any server. This includes:

  • Your vocabulary state (every word you've seen, tapped, or marked, and how well Logia thinks you know it)
  • Your reading progress in each book (the page you're on, paragraphs you've seen)
  • Your CEFR self-declared level and quiz answers
  • The list of books you have open

None of this leaves your device. There are no Logia accounts and no Logia server that stores your reading data — there is nowhere for it to be uploaded to.

If your iPhone has iCloud Backup switched on, these files are included in your device's normal encrypted Apple backup, like any other app's data. That is what lets your vocabulary and progress return if you reinstall Logia or set up a new phone. The backup is between you and Apple; we have no access to it and no way to read it. If you delete the app and have no iCloud backup, this data is gone for good.

Downloading books

Books are not included in the app itself — the app downloads them as you open them, from our file storage on Cloudflare R2. This is plain file hosting: it holds the book files and nothing about you. There is no account, no login, and nothing identifying you is attached to the request.

As with any download from any website, Cloudflare's servers necessarily see the request itself — your device's IP address and which file it asked for — in order to send the file back, and may log it briefly for security and abuse prevention. We don't build reading profiles from it and we don't combine it with anything else. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Anonymous usage analytics

To understand which features people use, Logia sends a small number of anonymous events to TelemetryDeck, a privacy-focused analytics provider based in Germany. These events include things like "a book was opened", "a page was turned", "a word was tapped", and the time you spent in the app per session.

What is not sent:

  • Your name, email, or any identifier that could be tied back to you
  • Your IP address (TelemetryDeck does not store it)
  • Advertising or marketing identifiers (no IDFA, no cookies)

TelemetryDeck assigns a one-way hashed identifier per app install so that repeat sessions from the same install can be counted as one user, but this identifier cannot be reversed into anything that identifies you. You can read TelemetryDeck's own privacy policy at telemetrydeck.com/privacy.

How to turn analytics off

Open Settings → Help improve Logia in the app and switch it off. Analytics are on by default; the switch is a real kill switch, not a preference we consult later. Switching it off stops every event immediately, and from the next time you open the app the analytics SDK is not started at all — so not even the session pings it would otherwise send on its own are sent.

Subscriptions and payment

Logia's subscription is sold through Apple's In-App Purchase system. Apple handles the entire transaction: we never see or receive your card details, billing address, or Apple Account. Apple's own privacy policy governs the payment.

All Logia receives is the answer to a single yes/no question, checked on your device — does this device have an active subscription? That check decides whether the full library is unlocked. There is no purchase history, no receipt, and no subscriber list held by us, and nothing about your subscription is attached to the usage analytics described above. Apple provides us with aggregate, anonymous sales reports, which cannot identify individual buyers.

Early-access sign-up (this website)

The "Request early access" button on this website opens a Google Form. If you fill it in, your answers — your email and a few optional questions about what you'd like to read — are collected via Google Forms into a Google Sheet on our Google account, so we can send you a TestFlight invite and occasional updates about the beta. That's the only thing it's used for; we don't sell it or add it to any third-party marketing list. Google processes the submission under Google's privacy policy. Ask us to remove you at any time by emailing ekaterina.gasparian@gmail.com and we'll delete it.

Third parties

Apple
If you installed Logia via the App Store or TestFlight, Apple receives standard install and crash data on Logia's behalf. See apple.com/legal/privacy.
TelemetryDeck
Anonymous product analytics as described above. See telemetrydeck.com/privacy.
Cloudflare
Hosts this website and the book files the app downloads, as described above. See Cloudflare's privacy policy.
Google Forms
Early-access sign-ups submitted on this website are collected via Google Forms / Google Sheets, as described above. See Google's privacy policy.
Project Gutenberg
Some of the books in Logia are public-domain texts from Project Gutenberg and its German sibling projekt-gutenberg.org; others are original works written for Logia. Either way, these sources are never contacted by the app — every book is downloaded from our own file storage, as described above.

Children

Logia is rated 9+ and does not knowingly collect any personal data from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a material way, the "Last updated" date above will change and a note will appear on the app's next launch.
